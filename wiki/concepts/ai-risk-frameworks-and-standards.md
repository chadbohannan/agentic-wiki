# AI Risk Frameworks and Standards

Voluntary frameworks and standards give AI governance a shared vocabulary and process: the NIST AI Risk Management Framework and its generative AI profile, ISO/IEC 42001, and IEEE's 7000 series and Ethically Aligned Design. None of them is law, unlike the horizontal [EU AI Act](eu-ai-act.md) or sector rules such as [FDA AI-Enabled Medical Devices](fda-ai-enabled-medical-devices.md), but regulators, auditors and procurement teams reference them. For an interview, know their names and one-line purposes, and claim no status beyond what each issuer states.

## The frameworks

**NIST AI RMF 1.0** (released 2023-01-26) is a voluntary framework organised around four functions: Govern, Map, Measure and Manage. Its cross-sector companion for generative AI is the Generative AI Profile, NIST-AI-600-1 (2024-07-26). NIST's page says the framework is being revised under the White House AI Action Plan, and notes an April 2026 concept note for a critical-infrastructure profile **[web]**.

**ISO/IEC 42001:2023** is the first international standard for an AI management system. It follows ISO's harmonised management-system structure (clauses 4–10) and has a control annex chosen through a Statement of Applicability. Accredited bodies certify an organisation's management system, not an individual model or agent, on a three-year cycle with annual surveillance audits. The certification scheme sits around the standard rather than in its text. As of 2026, it is not a harmonised standard under the EU AI Act, so it confers no presumption of conformity, according to standards bodies and certifiers rather than ISO itself **[web]**.

**IEEE.** The Ethically Aligned Design initiative, which the study guide cites and which involved more than 700 experts, fed the IEEE 7000 series **[sourced, web]**. The listed standards include:
- **7000:** a process for addressing ethical concerns during system design;
- **7001:** transparency of autonomous systems;
- **P7002:** data privacy process;
- **7003:** algorithmic bias considerations;
- **P7009:** fail-safe design of autonomous systems.

## Where they fit

NIST and ISO supply the process and governance vocabulary auditors expect, mapping risks, measuring them and managing them through a management system; the technical controls they organise are those in [Agent Security Threats and Controls](agent-security-threats-and-controls.md), and the human side those in [Human Oversight and Accountability](human-oversight-and-accountability.md). The IEEE 7000 series supplies narrower process standards. The [EU AI Act](eu-ai-act.md) is binding, and the others help show diligence towards it.

## Interview Q&A

**What are the four NIST AI RMF functions?** Govern, Map, Measure and Manage **[web]**.

**Which are voluntary and which mandatory?** The NIST RMF, ISO 42001 and the IEEE standards are voluntary. The EU AI Act is binding law **[web]**.

**What is ISO 42001?** A certifiable standard for an AI management system. It certifies the organisation's processes, not a specific model **[web]**.

**Which IEEE standards cover bias and transparency?** 7003 covers bias and 7001 covers transparency **[web]**.
